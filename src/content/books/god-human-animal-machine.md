I first heard about *God, Human, Animal, Machine* on The Gray Area podcast in 2023, and it opened a door into the philosophy and history surrounding artificial intelligence. O'Gieblyn moves between contemporary ideas such as Nick Bostrom's simulation argument and much older debates about materialism and the nature of mind. I was still new to the history of AI when I read it, so discovering how modern computing inherits old philosophical questions was genuinely exciting. I return to the book because it helps me resist treating AI as a purely technical subject; it is also a story about meaning, belief, and what we think a human being is.
